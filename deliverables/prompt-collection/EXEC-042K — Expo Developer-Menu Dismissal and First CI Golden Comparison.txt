## 5. Implement safe Expo overlay recognition

Add a bounded, reusable check for the exact Expo developer-menu/onboarding overlay.

Use native Appium/UiAutomator2 accessibility information.

Do not use coordinate-based tapping.

Do not recognize the overlay solely because a generic `Continue` button exists.

### Recognition requirements

Combine distinctive evidence such as:

- Expo development-client title or developer-menu text.
- Relevant runtime/development-build information.
- The identifiable onboarding explanation.
- Its associated `Continue` control.

Avoid depending exclusively on the literal runtime version `exposdk:57.0.0`; that value may change after a future dependency upgrade.

Require sufficient independent evidence to distinguish the Expo overlay from normal application content.

### Required behavior

**Overlay present and unambiguous:**

- Identify the unique intended `Continue` control.
- Click once.
- Wait for the overlay to disappear.
- Confirm the developer-menu controls are no longer obstructing the foreground hierarchy.
- Resume strict scenario identity verification.

**Overlay absent:**

- Do nothing.
- Continue the existing scenario verification.

**Ambiguous screen:**

- Do not click.
- Preserve diagnostics.
- Report the ambiguity.

**Click fails:**

- Preserve diagnostics.
- Report the interaction failure.
- Do not silently retry indefinitely.

**Overlay remains after dismissal:**

- Fail with a meaningful reason.
- Preserve screenshot, hierarchy and application state.

An explicitly identified `Close` control may be considered as a narrowly justified fallback, but only if it clearly belongs to the confirmed Expo overlay.

Do not introduce arbitrary Back-button presses, screen swipes, blind taps or generic dialog dismissal.

             |                   |

### Timing requirements

- The overlay never appears.
- The intended marker appears normally.
- The app crashes or becomes unresponsive.
